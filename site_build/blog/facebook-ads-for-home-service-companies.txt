12:T1e5c,<p>Facebook and Instagram ads work for home service companies when you treat them as a way to start conversations with homeowners who are not searching yet, then follow up fast. The setup that works for most contractors is a simple offer, real photos and videos of your crew and finished jobs, either Meta lead forms (more volume, lower intent) or a landing page (fewer, more serious leads), a budget large enough to test, and tracking that tells Meta which leads became booked jobs. The ads are only half the system. Speed and quality of follow up decide whether those leads turn into revenue.</p>
<h2>When Meta ads make sense for home services</h2>
<p>Google search catches people who already know they need you. Meta reaches people while they scroll, before they search. That makes Meta strongest for:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Projects people plan:</strong> roofing, epoxy garage floors, remodeling, landscaping, solar, windows, fencing.</li>
<li><strong>Recurring services:</strong> house cleaning, lawn care, pest control.</li>
<li><strong>Seasonal pushes:</strong> AC tune ups before summer, furnace checks before winter, gutter cleaning in fall.</li>
</ul>
<p>It is usually weaker for emergencies. Nobody waits to scroll Instagram when their water heater bursts.</p>
<h2>Lead forms vs landing pages</h2>
<p>This is the first big decision.</p>
<h3>Meta instant forms (lead forms)</h3>
<p>The form opens inside Facebook or Instagram, already filled in with the person&#39;s name, email and phone.</p>
<p><strong>Pros:</strong> cheap leads, high volume, easy on mobile.
<strong>Cons:</strong> lower intent. Some people tap through without reading, some use old phone numbers, and many forget they submitted.</p>
<p><strong>How to improve quality:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Choose the &quot;Higher intent&quot; form type, which adds a review step before submitting.</li>
<li>Add one or two qualifying questions, like &quot;What type of project?&quot; or &quot;Do you own your home?&quot;</li>
<li>Use a short intro that sets expectations: &quot;A team member will call you within 15 minutes to schedule a free estimate.&quot;</li>
<li>Text and call every lead within minutes. Lead form leads cool off very fast.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Landing pages</h3>
<p>The ad sends people to a page on your website with a form or booking calendar.</p>
<p><strong>Pros:</strong> higher intent, more information before they contact you, more control over the message.
<strong>Cons:</strong> higher cost per lead, depends on a fast page that loads well on phones.</p>
<p><strong>What a good landing page has:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>A headline that matches the ad.</li>
<li>Real photos of your work, not stock images.</li>
<li>Reviews from local customers.</li>
<li>A short form, or a calendar to book an estimate directly.</li>
<li>Your phone number as a tap to call button.</li>
<li>Fast loading on a phone.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Which to start with</h3>
<p>If your team calls leads fast and can handle volume, start with lead forms and add qualifying questions. If you have limited time and want fewer, more serious leads, start with a landing page. Many companies end up running both and comparing cost per booked job, not cost per lead.</p>
<h2>Creative that works for home services</h2>
<p>Creative is the image or video people see. On Meta it matters more than targeting. What tends to work for contractors:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Before and after photos</strong> of real jobs. A cracked, stained garage floor next to a finished epoxy floor stops the scroll.</li>
<li><strong>Short videos of the owner talking to the camera.</strong> Thirty to sixty seconds, filmed on a phone, explaining a common problem and how you fix it. People hire people.</li>
<li><strong>Process videos.</strong> The crew grinding the concrete, laying sod, tearing off shingles. Real work is interesting to watch.</li>
<li><strong>Customer reviews</strong> shown as a simple graphic or a short video testimonial.</li>
<li><strong>Local cues.</strong> Mention the area you serve. &quot;Boise homeowners&quot; or a recognizable local view can help people feel the ad is meant for them.</li>
</ul>
<p>Write the ad text like you talk. Lead with the problem or the result, say what the next step is, and keep it honest. Avoid fake urgency and exaggerated claims.</p>
<h3>A sample ad for an epoxy floor company</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Tired of a stained, dusty garage floor?</p>
<p>We install epoxy and polyaspartic floors across the Treasure Valley. Most garages are done in a day or two.</p>
<p>Tap below for a free estimate. We&#39;ll text you within minutes to set a time.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Plan to refresh creative regularly. When the same ads run for weeks to the same audience, results usually fade.</p>
<h2>Targeting</h2>
<p>Meta&#39;s delivery system has become good at finding buyers on its own, so targeting is simpler than it used to be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Target by location: the ZIP codes or radius you actually serve.</li>
<li>Keep age and interest targeting broad unless you have a reason to narrow it.</li>
<li>Exclude existing customers when running new customer offers.</li>
<li>Let creative do the targeting. A video about roof replacement finds people who care about roofs.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Budgets: how to think about them</h2>
<p>There is no correct budget for everyone. A practical way to set one:</p>
<ol>
<li>Decide what a booked job is worth to you in gross profit.</li>
<li>Decide what you can afford to pay to get one.</li>
<li>Estimate your funnel: how many leads become appointments, and how many appointments become jobs.</li>
<li>Work backward to a budget that gives Meta enough results to learn.</li>
</ol>
<p><em>Illustrative numbers only.</em> Say an epoxy floor job averages $4,500 with $2,000 of gross profit, and you are willing to pay up to $500 in ad spend to get one. If you close about one in four estimates, and about half of leads book an estimate, you need about eight leads per job. That means you can pay up to around $60 per lead. To get 30 leads in a month at that cost, you would plan about $1,800 of ad spend.</p>
<p>Meta&#39;s ad delivery tends to stabilize after a campaign gets enough conversions. Small budgets spread across many ad sets often never get there, so start with one or two campaigns and a focused set of ads.</p>
<h2>Tracking: tell Meta which leads were good</h2>
<p>Meta optimizes toward whatever you tell it is a conversion. If you only report lead form submissions, it will find more people who fill out forms, including low quality ones.</p>
<p>Better:</p>
<ul>
<li>Install the Meta pixel on your site and set up the Conversions API.</li>
<li>Connect your CRM so that when a lead becomes &quot;appointment booked&quot; or &quot;job sold,&quot; that event is sent back to Meta.</li>
<li>Use Meta&#39;s lead optimization options that use CRM data, such as conversion leads, once you have enough volume.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is the single biggest improvement for most home service ad accounts. Our post on tracking leads from click to closed sale explains the setup.</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Slow follow up.</strong> A lead form lead called the next day is often lost.</li>
<li><strong>Stock photos.</strong> Homeowners can tell.</li>
<li><strong>Judging after three days.</strong> Give campaigns time and enough budget to gather data.</li>
<li><strong>Measuring cost per lead only.</strong> Cheap leads that never book are expensive.</li>
<li><strong>Boosting posts</strong> instead of running proper campaigns with a clear goal.</li>
</ul>
