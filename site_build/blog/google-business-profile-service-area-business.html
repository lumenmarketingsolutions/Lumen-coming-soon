Google Business Profile for service area businesses: how to rank in the map pack without showing your address

November 14, 2026 · 6 min read · Lumen Marketing

If your business goes to customers instead of customers coming to you, Google wants you to hide your address and set a service area instead. You can still rank in the map pack this way. Google ranks local results on three things, relevance, distance and prominence, and a service area business can work on all three: pick the right categories, list your real services, earn steady reviews, keep your profile active and build a website that backs it all up.

This guide covers the rules for service area businesses, how to set up the profile correctly, and the work that actually moves rankings. We use the Boise area as an example, but the same steps apply anywhere in the US.

What counts as a service area business

Google's guidelines describe a service area business as one that visits or delivers to customers at their locations. Plumbers, HVAC companies, roofers, cleaners, landscapers, mobile detailers and epoxy floor installers usually fit here.

Some businesses are hybrids. A med spa or a showroom that receives customers at its address and also travels to them can show the address and set a service area.

The rules you need to follow

These come from Google's Business Profile guidelines and help pages. Read them yourself before you make changes, since Google updates them from time to time.

  • Hide your address if you do not serve customers there. Google's own example is a plumber who runs the business from home. That address should be removed from public view.
  • One profile per business. A service area business without a storefront is allowed one profile. Separate locations with their own staff and service areas can each have their own.
  • No virtual offices unless staffed. A virtual office does not qualify unless it is staffed during business hours. P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes are not acceptable addresses.
  • Service area size. You can list up to 20 service areas by city, ZIP code or region. Google advises that the overall area should not extend much more than about two hours of driving from your base, though some businesses can justify more.
  • Business name must be your real name. Adding keywords or cities to the name, like "Boise Roofing Experts | Best Roof Repair," breaks the guidelines and can get the profile suspended.

How to set it up, step by step

  1. Create or claim the profile at business.google.com using an email you own, not an employee's or an agency's personal account.
  2. Enter your real business name exactly as it appears on your signage, trucks and paperwork.
  3. Choose your primary category carefully. This is one of the strongest relevance signals you control. A roofer should pick "Roofing contractor," not "Contractor." Add secondary categories only for services you actually provide.
  4. Enter your address for verification, then choose not to show it to customers.
  5. Add your service areas. For a Boise company that could be Boise, Meridian, Nampa, Eagle, Kuna, Star and Caldwell. List the places you actually want work from.
  6. Complete verification. Google may ask for a video showing your equipment, vehicle, tools or proof of management. Have your branded truck or work materials ready.
  7. Fill in every field: hours, phone number (a tracking number is fine if your real number is listed as an additional phone), website link, services with short descriptions, and the business description.

What actually helps you rank

Google says plainly that you cannot pay for a better local ranking. What you can do is improve the three factors it names.

Relevance

Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Help it by:

  • Picking the most accurate primary category and a few honest secondary ones.
  • Listing each service you offer in the Services section, using the words customers type, like "AC repair," "furnace replacement" and "ductless mini split installation."
  • Linking your profile to a website that has a dedicated page for each main service and a page for each main city you serve.

Distance

Distance is how far your business is from the person searching. For service area businesses, Google still knows where you are based even with the address hidden. You will usually rank most easily near your base. Ranking in a town 30 miles away is harder, and stuffing service areas does not change that. City pages on your website, reviews from customers in those towns and local links help you compete further out.

Prominence

Prominence is how well known your business is. Google says reviews and ratings play a part, along with links and mentions around the web. In practice this means:

  • A steady flow of reviews. Ask every happy customer, ideally by text with a direct link, right after the job is done. Twenty reviews spread over a year look healthier than twenty in one week.
  • Replying to every review, good or bad, in a calm and specific way.
  • Consistent name, phone and website across directories like Yelp, Angi, the BBB, Nextdoor and your local chamber of commerce.
  • Local links, such as sponsoring a youth team, joining the Boise Metro Chamber or getting listed by suppliers and manufacturers you are certified with.

Activity that keeps the profile healthy

  • Photos. Upload real job photos regularly: before and after shots, your crew, your trucks. Avoid stock images.
  • Posts. Share updates, seasonal offers and recent projects. Posts are a simple way to show the profile is active.
  • Questions and answers. Answer common questions in plain words, such as whether you offer financing or free estimates.
  • Messages and calls. Respond quickly. A profile that sends calls to a full voicemail box wastes the ranking you worked for.

Common mistakes that cost rankings

  • Showing a home address when you do not serve customers there.
  • Keywords or city names in the business name.
  • Creating a second profile for another city without a real staffed location there.
  • Using a virtual office or mailbox address.
  • Buying reviews or offering discounts in exchange for reviews. Both violate Google's policies.
  • Changing the name, category and address all at once, which can trigger a new verification.

A simple monthly checklist

  • Ask every completed job for a review
  • Reply to every new review
  • Upload 5 to 10 real job photos
  • Publish at least two posts
  • Check hours, phone and holiday hours
  • Review the Performance tab for calls, direction requests and website clicks
  • Check that your tracking number still rings through

How to measure it

Use the Performance section of your profile to see calls, website clicks and the searches that found you. If you use a call tracking number on the profile, tag those calls as coming from Google Business Profile in your CRM so you can see which of them turned into booked jobs. Rankings are useful, but booked jobs from the profile are the number that counts.
