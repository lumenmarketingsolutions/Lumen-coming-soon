Database reactivation: how to turn old leads and past customers into booked jobs

November 14, 2026 · 6 min read · Lumen Marketing

Database reactivation means reaching back out to old leads and past customers who already know your business, usually by text and email, with a simple and relevant message that invites them to book. For most service businesses it is the fastest and cheapest way to fill the calendar, because you are not paying for new attention. The steps are: export and clean your list, confirm you have permission to contact each person, segment by who they are, send a short personal message with a reason to act now, and have someone ready to answer replies quickly.

Why it works

Every service business has a list it is not using. It holds leads who asked for a quote and went quiet, customers who had a furnace tune up two years ago, and patients who came in for Botox once and never rebooked. These people already trust you more than a stranger who sees an ad.

Many of them did not say no. They got busy, the timing was off or they forgot your name. A well timed message reminds them.

Step 1: Gather your list

Pull contacts from everywhere they live:

  • Your CRM or field service software (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan and similar)
  • Your booking system or med spa EMR
  • QuickBooks or invoicing records
  • Old spreadsheets and email inboxes
  • Lead form exports from Facebook and Google

Merge them into one list with name, phone, email, what they asked about or bought, and the date of last contact.

Step 2: Clean it

  • Remove duplicates.
  • Remove anyone who opted out before. Never message them again.
  • Remove landlines from text lists.
  • Remove anyone who is clearly a bad fit, such as outside your service area.
  • Run emails through a verification tool to catch dead addresses, which protects your sender reputation.

Step 3: Check consent before you text

This step is not optional. Under the TCPA, automated marketing texts should only go to people who gave prior express written consent to receive them. Past customers and old leads do not automatically qualify. Ask yourself for each group:

  • Did they check a box or sign something agreeing to marketing texts from you?
  • Is your number registered for A2P 10DLC with a use case that covers marketing?

If you are not sure you have text consent for a group, reach them by email first (email follows different rules under CAN-SPAM) or by a personal phone call. An email can invite them to opt in to texts. Talk to an attorney if you plan to text a large list without clear consent records. The TCPA allows statutory damages of $500 per violation, more if willful, and that adds up fast.

Step 4: Segment

One message for everyone gets weak results. Split the list so each message feels personal:

  • Past customers by service and date (for example, "AC tune up in 2024").
  • Quoted but not sold, with the project they asked about.
  • Leads who never booked an estimate or consult.
  • Med spa patients by treatment and time since last visit.

Step 5: Write short, personal messages

The best reactivation texts are short, sound like a real person and ask an easy question. They do not look like a coupon blast.

Sample texts

Old leads (home services):

Hi [Name], it's [Rep] from [Company]. You asked us about [service] a while back. Is that still something you're looking at? Reply STOP to opt out.

Past HVAC customers:

Hi [Name], [Company] here. It's been about a year since your last tune up. Want us to get you on the schedule before winter? Reply STOP to opt out.

Quoted roofing or epoxy projects:

Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with [Company]. We still have your quote for the [project] on file. We have a few openings next month. Want me to hold one for you? Reply STOP to opt out.

Med spa patients:

Hi [Name], it's [Name] at [Med Spa]. It's been about [X] months since your last [treatment]. Would you like me to send a few times this week? Reply STOP to opt out.

Notice that none of these lead with a discount. A simple question gets replies. If you do make an offer, make it real and time limited, like opening a few extra slots, a free add on or a seasonal service window.

Sample email

Subject: Still thinking about your [project]?

Hi [Name],

A while back you asked us about [project]. I wanted to check in personally.

If the timing wasn't right then, no problem. If you're still considering it, we have some availability in [month] and I'd be glad to update your quote.

You can pick a time here: [booking link]. Or just reply to this email.

[Name], [Company]

Step 6: Send in batches

Do not send to your whole list at once. Send in batches of a few hundred per day. This lets your team answer replies quickly, keeps your phone number in good standing with carriers and lets you adjust the message if the first batch does not land.

Send texts during the day in the recipient's time zone, and never before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m.

Step 7: Handle replies fast

Reactivation creates a burst of conversations. Plan for it:

  • Assign one person, or an AI agent with clear rules, to reply within minutes during business hours.
  • Have a booking link and real available times ready.
  • Tag replies in your CRM: interested, booked, not now, wrong number, opted out.
  • Process opt outs immediately and automatically.

A reply that sits for a day loses most of its value.

Step 8: Follow up once or twice

People who do not respond to the first text may respond to a second one a few days later. Keep it short:

Hi [Name], just bumping this in case it got buried. Still interested in [service]?

After two or three attempts, stop and move them to a long term email list.

What to measure

  • Delivered messages
  • Reply rate
  • Booked appointments
  • Show rate
  • Revenue from booked jobs
  • Opt out rate

If opt outs are high, your list is too old, the message feels like spam or you are texting people who never agreed to it.

How often to run it

Many service businesses run a reactivation campaign once a quarter, timed to seasons: AC tune ups in spring, furnace checks in fall, holiday touch ups for med spas. Between campaigns, keep the list fresh by collecting text consent from every new customer.
