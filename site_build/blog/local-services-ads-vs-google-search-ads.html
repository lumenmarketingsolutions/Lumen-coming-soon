Local Services Ads vs Google Search ads: which should a local service business start with?

November 14, 2026 · 6 min read · Lumen Marketing

Most local service businesses in an eligible category should start with Google Local Services Ads (LSAs), because you pay per lead instead of per click, they show at the very top of many local searches, and setup is mostly verification and reviews rather than campaign building. Add Google Search ads once LSAs are running, or start with Search first if your category is not eligible for LSAs, you need more control over which services and areas you show for, or LSA volume in your area is too low. Many established home service companies run both.

How Local Services Ads work

LSAs are the ads that appear at the top of some local searches with your business name, star rating, and a badge. When someone taps your listing, they can call you, message you or in some categories request a booking.

Key points:

  • You pay per lead, not per click. A lead is a call, message or booking request that comes through the ad.
  • Google verifies your business. Depending on your category and location, this can include license checks, insurance, background checks and reviews.
  • The badge is now "Google Verified." In October 2025 Google replaced the older Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified badges with a single Google Verified badge, and discontinued the money back guarantee that came with Google Guaranteed.
  • Ranking depends on things like your bid, how relevant your business is to the search, how quickly you respond (Google notes that missed calls can count against you), your rating and number of reviews, your photos and your verification status.
  • You can give lead feedback, and Google has a process for credits on leads that do not fit your business.

Eligibility

LSAs are only available for certain categories, and some categories are limited to certain states. Common home service categories like HVAC, plumbing and roofing are on Google's US list. Many professional categories, such as lawyers and some financial services, are eligible in some markets. Google's own help center has an eligibility checker. The simplest test is to start signing up and see whether your category and service area are offered.

Getting set up well

  • Complete every verification step quickly. Pending accounts can show below fully verified ones.
  • Choose only the job types you want. If you do not want small repair calls, turn them off.
  • Set your hours honestly and answer the phone during them. Google counts responsiveness in ranking, and missed calls can hurt your position.
  • Keep reviews coming in. Your Google Business Profile reviews carry over.
  • Mark leads as booked in the LSA dashboard so you can see what turns into work.

How Google Search ads work

Search ads are text ads that show on Google results for keywords you choose, like "furnace repair Boise" or "epoxy garage floor near me." You pay when someone clicks. You control the keywords, the ad text, the landing page, the schedule and the bids.

Search ads give you more control:

  • Choose the exact services you want to advertise and leave out the rest.
  • Write your own message and send people to a landing page built for that service.
  • Use negative keywords to avoid searches you do not want, like "DIY," "jobs," "free" or "cheap."
  • Reach categories LSAs do not cover, and services that fall outside the LSA job types.

The tradeoff is more work. Search ads need keyword research, ad writing, landing pages, conversion tracking and regular management. And you pay for clicks even when the person never calls.

Which to start with

Start with LSAs if:

  • Your category is eligible in your area.
  • You can pass verification and have at least a handful of solid reviews.
  • Someone answers the phone during your listed hours.
  • You want leads without building a full campaign first.

Start with Search ads if:

  • Your category is not eligible for LSAs.
  • You want to promote specific high value services, like a full roof replacement or a full garage floor system, rather than every job type.
  • You have a good landing page and conversion tracking ready.
  • LSA volume in your area is too low to fill your schedule.

Run both if you have the budget and capacity. LSAs often bring the most ready to buy calls, and Search ads let you expand into specific services and towns.

What about Performance Max?

Performance Max campaigns run across Search, Maps, YouTube and other Google surfaces with automated targeting. They can work, but they give you less visibility into where the money goes. For most local service businesses, it is easier to learn what works with LSAs and a focused Search campaign first, then test Performance Max once conversion tracking is solid.

Tracking that makes both work

Whichever you pick, measure booked jobs.

  • For LSAs, use the dashboard to mark lead outcomes and dispute leads that do not fit. Record each LSA call in your CRM.
  • For Search, set up conversion tracking for calls and forms, and import booked and sold outcomes back into Google Ads so its bidding learns what a good lead looks like.

Our post on tracking leads from click to closed sale covers the details.

A quick example

Illustrative numbers only.

A plumbing company in Nampa spends $1,500 a month on LSAs and receives about 40 leads. They book 25 jobs. Their cost per booked job is $60.

They add a Search campaign for water heater replacement, a higher value service, at $1,500 a month. It brings in 30 leads and 10 installs. The cost per install is $150, but each install is worth far more than an average service call.

Both numbers are useful because they are measured in booked work.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving every LSA job type turned on, including ones you do not want.
  • Not answering LSA calls, which can lower your position.
  • Running Search ads to the homepage instead of a service specific page.
  • No negative keywords.
  • Judging either channel by cost per lead alone.
