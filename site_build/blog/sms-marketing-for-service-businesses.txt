12:T2421,<p>To text customers from a business phone number in the US, you need three things in place. First, your number must be registered for A2P 10DLC through your texting provider, which means registering your business (the &quot;brand&quot;) and how you will text (the &quot;campaign&quot;). Second, you need the right consent: clear permission to text, and for marketing texts, prior express written consent collected with proper disclosure. Third, you need to follow the basic rules, like honoring STOP right away, identifying your business and respecting quiet hours. Do those well and texting becomes one of the most useful tools a service business has.</p>
<p><em>This is general information, not legal advice. Texting law changes, and some states add stricter rules. Check with an attorney for your specific situation.</em></p>
<h2>What A2P 10DLC is</h2>
<p>A2P means &quot;application to person,&quot; which is any text sent from software instead of a person tapping on a phone. 10DLC means &quot;10 digit long code,&quot; a normal local phone number. US carriers require businesses that send A2P texts from local numbers to register through The Campaign Registry (TCR). Unregistered traffic gets filtered, blocked or charged extra carrier fees.</p>
<p>You register through your texting provider, such as Twilio or a platform built on it like HighLevel. There are two parts.</p>
<h3>Brand registration</h3>
<p>This is your business identity: legal name, EIN, address, website and contact details. The details must match what the IRS has on file for your EIN exactly. A mismatch is one of the most common reasons registrations fail.</p>
<p>Sole proprietors without an EIN can register as a sole proprietor brand, with lower limits.</p>
<h3>Campaign registration</h3>
<p>This describes what you will send. You choose a use case (for example marketing, customer care, account notifications or mixed), write a description, give sample messages and explain how people opt in. Carriers review this manually.</p>
<p>Campaigns often get rejected for:</p>
<ul>
<li>A website with no privacy policy, or a privacy policy that does not say you will not share mobile numbers with third parties for marketing.</li>
<li>Sample messages that do not match the use case, or that leave out your business name and opt out language.</li>
<li>An opt in description that does not show where and how people agree to receive texts.</li>
<li>A website that does not match the business name you registered.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Fees</h3>
<p>Fees are set by TCR and the carriers and passed through by your provider, so they change. As of late 2026, Twilio lists a one time brand registration fee of $4.50, a one time $41.50 fee for Standard brand vetting, and a $15 one time campaign vetting fee, plus a monthly campaign fee that depends on the use case. Your provider may add its own markup. Check your provider&#39;s current pricing page before budgeting.</p>
<h2>Consent under the TCPA</h2>
<p>The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) is the federal law that covers calls and texts. The details are complicated, but the practical version for a service business is this.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Informational texts</strong> a customer asked for, like appointment confirmations and &quot;your tech is on the way,&quot; generally need the customer to have given you their number for that purpose.</li>
<li><strong>Marketing texts,</strong> like promotions, seasonal offers and reactivation campaigns sent with automated software, should only go to people who gave prior express written consent. That means a clear agreement (a checkbox or signed form, which can be electronic) that says they agree to receive marketing texts from your business at that number, that consent is not a condition of purchase, and that message and data rates may apply.</li>
</ul>
<p>The TCPA lets individuals sue. The statute sets damages at $500 per violation, and a court can triple that for willful or knowing violations. Because each text can count as a violation, class actions over texting can become very expensive. This is why consent matters more than any clever message.</p>
<p>In January 2025 a federal appeals court struck down an FCC rule that would have required &quot;one to one&quot; consent for each specific seller. The core consent rules above still apply.</p>
<h3>A sample consent checkbox for your forms</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>☐ I agree to receive marketing and appointment text messages from [Company Name] at the number provided. Consent is not a condition of purchase. Message frequency varies. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out or HELP for help. See our [Privacy Policy] and [Terms].</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Leave the box unchecked by default. Store a record of when and where each person agreed.</p>
<h2>Opt outs</h2>
<p>Under FCC rules, people can revoke consent by any reasonable means, and replies such as &quot;stop,&quot; &quot;quit,&quot; &quot;end,&quot; &quot;revoke,&quot; &quot;opt out,&quot; &quot;cancel&quot; and &quot;unsubscribe&quot; are treated as valid opt outs. The rules require you to honor a revocation within a reasonable time, no more than ten business days. You may send one confirmation text with no marketing content, and a confirmation sent within five minutes is presumed fine.</p>
<p>The FCC adjusted parts of its revocation rules again in late 2026, so ask your attorney about the current version. The safe habit is simple: stop texting the moment someone asks, by any wording, and make sure your CRM does it automatically.</p>
<h2>Quiet hours and other basics</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Quiet hours.</strong> Federal rules bar telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the recipient&#39;s local time. Several states set tighter windows and limits on how many messages you can send. Florida and Oklahoma are well known examples. Many businesses simply keep marketing texts between about 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. local time.</li>
<li><strong>Identify yourself.</strong> Put your business name in the first text of any conversation.</li>
<li><strong>Do Not Call list.</strong> Marketing texts to numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry need proper consent.</li>
<li><strong>No purchased lists.</strong> Texting a bought list of numbers is the fastest route to a lawsuit and a blocked number.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What to text and when</h2>
<p>Here is a practical texting plan for a service business. Replace the brackets with your details.</p>
<h3>Right after a lead comes in</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with [Company]. Thanks for asking about [service]. What&#39;s a good time today for a quick call? Reply STOP to opt out.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Appointment confirmation</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>You&#39;re booked with [Company] on [Day] at [Time] for your [service]. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Day before reminder</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Reminder: [Tech] from [Company] will see you tomorrow between [window]. Anything we should know before we arrive?</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>On the way</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>[Tech] is on the way and should arrive in about 20 minutes.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Review request after the job</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Thanks for choosing [Company], [Name]! If we did a good job, would you leave a quick review? It helps a lot: [link]</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Estimate follow up (two days later)</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Hi [Name], just checking whether you had any questions on the estimate for [project]. Happy to walk through it.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>Seasonal reminder (marketing, needs written consent)</h3>
<blockquote>
<p>Hi [Name], [Company] here. Furnace season is coming. Want us to book your tune up before the first cold snap? Reply YES and we&#39;ll send times. Reply STOP to opt out.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>How often to text</h2>
<p>For marketing texts, less is more. One or two promotional messages a month is plenty for most service businesses. Transactional texts like reminders and confirmations can go out as needed, because customers want them.</p>
<p>Watch your opt out rate. If more than a small handful of people opt out after a campaign, the message or the timing was off.</p>
<h2>A setup checklist</h2>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Business details match your EIN records exactly</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Website shows business name, contact info, privacy policy and terms</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Privacy policy states mobile numbers are not shared for third party marketing</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Forms include an unchecked consent box with full disclosure</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Brand and campaign registered and approved</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> STOP, HELP and similar keywords handled automatically</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Marketing sends limited to daytime hours in the recipient&#39;s time zone</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Consent records stored in your CRM</li>
</ul>
