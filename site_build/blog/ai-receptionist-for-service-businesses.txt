12:T1cac,<p>An AI receptionist is software that answers calls, texts or website chats for your business, using a voice or written conversation that sounds natural. For service businesses it works best on repetitive, rules based tasks: answering after hours, replying to new leads in seconds, answering common questions, qualifying the job and booking appointments into your calendar. Keep humans in charge of anything involving judgment, money disputes, emergencies, medical or legal advice, and unhappy customers. The best setups use AI to make sure no lead waits, and pass the conversation to a person the moment it needs one.</p>
<h2>What AI agents are good at</h2>
<h3>Answering after hours and during busy times</h3>
<p>Calls and messages do not stop at 5 p.m. An AI agent can pick up when your team cannot, answer basic questions and book a time, instead of sending the caller to voicemail.</p>
<h3>Replying to new leads instantly</h3>
<p>When someone fills out a form or a Facebook lead form, an AI agent can text back within seconds, ask a couple of qualifying questions and offer appointment times. Fast replies matter because people often contact several companies.</p>
<h3>Answering common questions</h3>
<ul>
<li>What areas do you serve?</li>
<li>Do you offer free estimates?</li>
<li>What are your hours?</li>
<li>Do you offer financing?</li>
<li>How long does a garage floor take to cure?</li>
</ul>
<p>You write the approved answers. The agent uses them.</p>
<h3>Qualifying and booking</h3>
<p>The agent can collect the details your team needs: service type, address, project size, timeline and how they heard about you. It can then book into real calendar slots and update the CRM.</p>
<h3>Reminders and rescheduling</h3>
<p>Confirming appointments, handling simple reschedules and sending reminders are good tasks for automation.</p>
<h2>What to keep human</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Emergencies.</strong> A gas smell, flooding or a medical complication should go straight to a person and, where appropriate, emergency services. Configure the agent to recognize these and hand off immediately.</li>
<li><strong>Pricing judgment.</strong> The agent can share published price ranges. Custom quotes, discounts and negotiation belong to your team.</li>
<li><strong>Complaints and angry customers.</strong> People who are upset want a human. Route them fast.</li>
<li><strong>Medical, legal and financial advice.</strong> A med spa agent can explain what a consult involves, but questions about candidacy, side effects or medications must go to a licensed provider. The same goes for legal or lending questions at a law firm or lender.</li>
<li><strong>Final sales conversations</strong> on high ticket jobs. AI can book the estimate. Closing a $30,000 roof is your job.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Legal basics to know</h2>
<p><em>This is general information, not legal advice.</em></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Outbound AI voice calls are regulated.</strong> In February 2024 the FCC ruled that AI generated voices count as &quot;artificial&quot; voices under the TCPA. That means outbound calls using an AI voice need the same consent as prerecorded calls. Inbound calls, where the customer calls you, are a different situation, but check with counsel before using AI voice for any outbound calling.</li>
<li><strong>Texts follow texting rules.</strong> AI texting still needs A2P 10DLC registration, proper consent and opt out handling.</li>
<li><strong>Call recording laws vary by state.</strong> Some states require everyone on the call to consent to recording. Disclose that calls may be recorded.</li>
<li><strong>Be honest that it is AI.</strong> Several states have passed or are considering rules on disclosing AI in customer conversations. Disclosing is the simple, safe habit, and most customers do not mind when the agent is helpful.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to roll one out</h2>
<h3>1. Start with one job</h3>
<p>Pick one clear task, such as after hours call answering or instant replies to new leads. Get it working well before adding more.</p>
<h3>2. Write the knowledge base</h3>
<p>Give the agent your services, service area, hours, pricing ranges you are comfortable sharing, policies, FAQs and booking rules. Be specific. Vague instructions produce vague answers.</p>
<h3>3. Write the rules for handoff</h3>
<p>List exactly when the agent should stop and bring in a person:</p>
<ul>
<li>The customer asks for a human.</li>
<li>Any mention of emergency, injury, gas, flooding or a medical problem.</li>
<li>A complaint or refund request.</li>
<li>Questions outside the knowledge base.</li>
</ul>
<p>Decide how the handoff happens: a live transfer during business hours, a text to the on call tech, or a task in the CRM with an urgent tag.</p>
<h3>4. Connect it to your CRM and calendar</h3>
<p>The agent should book into real availability and save every conversation to the contact record, so your team sees the full history when they call back.</p>
<h3>5. Test it hard</h3>
<p>Before going live, call and text it yourself with real scenarios: a confused customer, an emergency, someone outside your area, someone asking for a price you do not publish. Fix every wrong answer.</p>
<h3>6. Review conversations every week</h3>
<p>Read transcripts. Look for wrong answers, missed handoffs and customers who got frustrated. Update the knowledge base. Plan to keep doing this for as long as the agent runs.</p>
<h2>A sample after hours text flow for an HVAC company</h2>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Customer:</strong> My furnace isn&#39;t turning on</p>
<p><strong>Agent:</strong> Sorry to hear that. I can help get a tech out. Is anyone in the home at risk from the cold right now, like infants, elderly family or anyone with health concerns? And do you smell gas at all?</p>
<p><strong>Customer:</strong> No gas smell, just cold</p>
<p><strong>Agent:</strong> Thanks. Our first opening is tomorrow between 8 and 10 a.m. Would that work? If you&#39;d rather talk to our on call tech tonight, just say so and I&#39;ll have them call you.</p>
</blockquote>
<p>If the customer had mentioned a gas smell, the agent would tell them to leave the home, call the gas company or 911, and alert the on call tech right away.</p>
<h2>What to measure</h2>
<ul>
<li>Share of after hours leads that get a reply within one minute</li>
<li>Booked appointments from AI conversations</li>
<li>Handoff rate and how fast humans respond after handoff</li>
<li>Complaints or bad reviews that mention the agent</li>
<li>Show rate for AI booked appointments compared with human booked ones</li>
</ul>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Letting the AI guess.</strong> If it does not know, it should say so and hand off.</li>
<li><strong>Hiding that it is AI.</strong> Customers feel tricked when they figure it out.</li>
<li><strong>No human backup.</strong> An agent that cannot reach a person in an emergency is a liability.</li>
<li><strong>Set and forget.</strong> Agents need regular review like any employee.</li>
<li><strong>Automating the wrong thing first.</strong> Start where leads are being lost today.</li>
</ul>
